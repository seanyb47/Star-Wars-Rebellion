# How the original rated its people

*Read out of Sean's **Star Wars Rebellion Unit Stats** sheet (Drive, `7 Seas`),
15 September, and set beside what we have built. Reference, not a plan —
nothing here has been applied.*

The original rates a character on **the same four abilities we do**: Diplomacy,
Espionage, Combat, Leadership. That is the parallel worth having, and it holds
all the way down. Where it stops holding is more interesting than where it does.

---

## 1. A range per ability, rolled per game

Every character carries two numbers per ability. Wedge Antilles is
`espionage 70–100`; a game gives him one figure in that band. This is our
base-and-swing rule wearing different clothes, and it arrived at the same place
independently, which is a good sign for the rule.

**But the spans are much wider than ours.** The original's bands are 30 to 50
points across — `0–30`, `70–100`, `0–50`, `80–100`. Ours are 20 (minor) and 40
(major). A Rebellion captain can be twice the spy in one game that he is in the
next; ours wobble.

## 2. The icons do not vary at all — and that is the opposite of our rule

| | second column | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Mon Mothma, Leia, Luke, Han, Palpatine, Vader | **0** | fixed, every game |
| the other 54 | 10 – 50 | rolled, every game |

Vader is always Vader: combat 100, leadership 100, no dice. The nobodies are
the lottery tickets.

**We do it the other way round.** Sean's rule of 15 September gives a *major*
character ±20 and a *minor* one ±10, so our icons vary twice as much as our
walk-ons. Both are defensible — ours says "the great are volatile", the
original's says "the great are reliable and the rank-and-file are a gamble" —
but they are opposite designs and we chose ours without knowing the original
had chosen the other. Worth a deliberate second look.

## 3. Above 100 is real, and rare

Two characters in sixty:

- **Leia Organa** — diplomacy **120**, fixed.
- **Thrawn** — leadership **100–130**.

That is it. Passing a hundred marks out exactly two people in the whole game as
operating outside the scale.

**Ours is far more common.** Measured over 80 worlds, **194** rolled scores came
out above 100. If passing a hundred is meant to feel like Leia and Thrawn, our
swing is too wide, our bases too high, or both.

## 4. The minor cast is archetypes, not individuals

Fifty-four non-iconic characters share **18 stat lines**, and one line covers
fifteen of them:

| count | dip | esp | com | lea | reads as |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 | 0–30 | 70–100 | 70–100 | 80–100 | the standard fighting officer |
| 8 | 70–100 | 0–50 | 0–50 | 80–100 | the diplomat-administrator |
| 5 | 0–30 | 90–100 | 80–100 | 0–30 | the spy who commands nothing |
| 4 | 0–30 | 0–50 | 0–50 | 80–100 | the pure general |

We do not need this and should not copy it: our cast is 26 people with names,
faces and bios, and every one is individually rated. The original needed
templates because it had sixty.

## 5. Faction shape — and the lesson for our opening

Means across all sixty, using the midpoint of each band:

| | diplomacy | espionage | combat | leadership |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Empire | 28.0 | 61.2 | 65.7 | 72.0 |
| Alliance | 36.7 | 68.3 | 69.7 | 71.8 |
| gap (A−E) | **+8.7** | **+7.2** | **+4.0** | **−0.2** |

**The Rebels are better at everything, and leadership is dead level.** The
Empire's advantage in the original is not its people — it is Star Destroyers,
stormtroopers and a Death Star. The Alliance has the better spies and the
better talkers and wins the long game with them.

That is very close to what Sean wants for us, with one difference: he wants the
Crown to carry a clear **leadership** edge, which the original does not give the
Empire. Our current split:

| | diplomacy | espionage | combat | leadership |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Crown | 58.0 | 56.6 | 66.9 | **78.9** |
| Brethren | **69.1** | **67.4** | 67.7 | 68.1 |

Same direction on diplomacy and espionage, a deliberate divergence on
leadership, and combat level where the original tilts Rebel.

**The load-bearing parallel is materiel.** The original makes the Empire
frightening with hulls, not officers — and our Crown is currently the *weaker*
navy on day one, 17 guns to the Brethren's 33. The original says the Imperial
side should feel heavy. Ours does not yet.

## 6. Three things the original has that we do not

**Admiral / Commander / General flags.** A character can only take a fleet if
flagged Admiral, or troops if flagged General. Leadership is a *number* and a
*permission*. This is the cheapest available answer to "put leaders on islands
to lead": make command a thing only some officers may do.

**Betrayal.** 51 of 60 can defect; the six icons cannot. Our bible has this as
Double Agents in §14.2 and it is unbuilt.

**Research as three specialties.** Ship, Troop and Facility research each have
their own base and variance, so a researcher is specialised. Bevel Lemelisk is
50/50/50 across all three and useless at everything else. We have one research
mission driven by espionage.

---

## What I would take from it

1. **Reconsider the variance inversion** (§2). It is a one-line change either
   way and the original's instinct is strong: the famous are constants, the
   obscure are dice.
2. **Make passing 100 rare** (§3). Two characters in sixty, not a fifth of all
   rolls.
3. **Let the Crown be heavy** (§5). The original's Empire is materiel; ours is
   currently out-gunned two to one on day one by the Lords' three hulls.
4. **Command as a permission, not just a number** (§6) — the most direct route
   to the suppression behaviour Sean described.
