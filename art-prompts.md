# Art Prompts — Master of the Seven Seas

Every subject the game needs, written to the agreed style. Paste a block
straight into an image generator; the style preamble is repeated in each so
they can be used one at a time without drift.

**Drop the results into `src/art/`** — see the README there for naming. Any
subject without a file keeps its drawn cameo, so the art can arrive in any
order and the game is never half-finished.

## The style, in one line

> cinematic stylized historical realism, hand-painted trading-card illustration, authentic late-17th/18th-century maritime detail, strong readable silhouette, dramatic natural lighting, textured painterly brushwork, rich but weathered colours, romantic adventure atmosphere, 80% historical realism and 20% dark nautical fantasy; never photorealistic, cartoonish, steampunk, high-fantasy or overly magical

## Technical

- **Portraits** 512×512 square, head and shoulders, subject centred. They are
  displayed inside a circle as small as 32px: keep the head large in frame,
  nothing important near a corner, no fine detail that will not survive.
- **Ships** 768×512 landscape, three-quarter view, full vessel in frame.
- **Islands** 768×512 landscape, seen from a low approach as if from a boat.
- **Scenes** 1024×432, the dispatch card's proportion.
- **WebP, quality ~82, under 120KB each.**
- Dark, moody key. The game's ground is near-black `#06161d`; art that is
  bright overall will float off the page.

## A note on faction colour

The interface reads allegiance from colour: **Imperium green, Confederacy red,
unaligned light blue.** Paintings carry a faction wash in the medallion, but
the uniform itself doing the work is much stronger. Keep Imperial coats
sea-green and Confederate sashes red wherever it is plausible.

# Dispatch scenes (7)

The card that stops you when something happens. These are the widest images and
the most cinematic — they are the game's only full-bleed art, so this is where
the style earns its keep.

The card tints itself in the colour of whichever side the news concerns, so
**leave the sky doing the least work**: a scene painted with a strong sky of
its own will fight the tint.

## Order to make them in

1. **Seven dispatch scenes.** Biggest on screen, fewest in number, most
   cinematic — the best return per image by a distance.
2. **Ten island archetypes.** Second most visible, and ten covers a hundred
   islands.
3. **Eight ships.**
4. **Twenty-six portraits**, majors before recruits. Last because they are
   displayed smallest and the drawn cameos already work at 32px.

Twenty-six portraits is also where the size problem lives: at 120KB each that
alone is 3MB against a 95KB game. Lazy loading needs building before that
batch lands, not after.
